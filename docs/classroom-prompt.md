# The classroom product brief

This is the product-focused part of the brief used for Learning Room, shared as a study reference. Wording and formatting have been lightly edited for readability. Private setup and deployment instructions are omitted.

## The brief

Act as a product manager. Build a solution for the following learning-centre problems, taking educational value, environmental responsibility, long-term sustainability, economics and social access into account.

### Problems to understand

Teachers in a learning centre work with students at different levels. Preparing separate customised notes takes time, and teachers need to understand what each learner needs.

Some students want more challenging questions and opportunities to earn bonus marks. Teachers want to encourage them to extend their thinking.

English covers many areas, including grammar, sentence structure, writing and listening. Students can struggle in different areas, which makes it difficult to follow their progress as a whole.

Teachers want to plan the structure of lessons, observe engagement, record learner performance and reflect on their own teaching after each class.

Learners may come from different schools and have different schedules. Teachers need to keep track of school backgrounds, arrange classes and coordinate availability.

### What the solution should consider

**Environmental responsibility.** Consider environmental, social and governance (ESG) factors. Identify what evidence would be needed to assess environmental benefit and support a relevant funding review.

**Pedagogy.** Build an education tool around suitable learning objectives, useful feedback and teacher judgement.

**Sustainability.** Plan how the content and product will be maintained, reviewed and improved over time.

**Economics.** Consider how the project could be supported financially, including a potential school or university collaboration. Make assumptions and unconfirmed support clear.

**Social access and community.** Consider how better class arrangements could reduce disruption for neighbours, and how learners with fewer resources or limited access to devices and opportunities could participate.

Connect the problems and features into a coherent product rather than treating them as unrelated screens.

## Questions to take into your own build

- Which requirements describe a user problem, and which describe a proposed solution?
- What is the smallest useful workflow you could build and test?
- How would a teacher know whether a learner needs more support or a different challenge?
- Which decisions should remain with a teacher?
- What would you measure before claiming time saved, learning improvement or environmental benefit?
- Who might be left out by your design, and what alternative could you offer?
- Which funding or institutional-support assumptions would need independent verification?

The demo is one interpretation of this brief. Your own version can make different choices if you can explain the people, evidence and constraints behind them.
